QUICK HELP
All twelve modules are live. Click one in the Signal Chain to open it; Learn explains every control.
  • Auto-Master: pick your song's style (top), an intensity and a target, press Auto-Master and play your whole song from the start. It finds the loudest part by itself, finishes a few seconds after the song ends (or click it again any time after 12 seconds to use what it heard), sets every module and explains why in Learn. The undo arrow puts everything back.
  • Auto Analyze: after Auto-Master, switch on AUTO ANALYZE at the top of Learn and keep mixing. While the song plays it listens; when it hears the hook or drop again and your mix has changed, Learn shows what Auto-Master would change now, one card per module (now → new). APPLY sets it (one undo step), KEEP MINE leaves it. It never changes anything by itself.
  • Undo / redo: the arrows at the top step back and forward through your changes one at a time (a knob move, a switch, an Auto-Master run, a snapshot). Point at an arrow to see what it will undo. Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y redoes (click inside Masterology first). Kept while the window is closed; not saved with the project.
  • Snapshots S1–S4: each keeps a whole sound (every setting and Tone Steady's target, plus its Auto-Master report). Click an empty slot to keep what you hear; click a stored one to switch to it (it glides, and undo goes back); STORE then a slot (or Shift-click) replaces it. Red = playing, S1• = changed since. They're not A/B and never change A/B, MATCH or the listening aids. Try Balanced in S1 vs Punchy in S2 with MATCH on: play each for 15 s once, then flip; MATCH remembers each one's level.
  • Match ref: click Match ref (next to the targets) and load a released song you want to sound like (WAV, AIFF, FLAC or MP3). Masterology measures its loudest part; then press Auto-Master and play your song: it aims for the reference's tone, loudness, width and low end instead of the genre's. The dashed line on the spectrum is the reference's tone. The × on the pill clears it.
  • Spectrum / History: the dotted line (e.g. Trap target) is the tone typical masters in your genre have: where your red master line sits above it, your master has more of that range (1–2 dB either way is normal); click its name to show or hide it. HISTORY (under the spectrum) shows your master's loudness over the last 2 minutes of playback, with the Maximizer's gain reduction hanging from the top (orange = more than 6 dB, heavy limiting).
  • Knobs: drag up/down. Hold Shift for fine control. Double-click to reset. Mouse wheel works too.
  • A / B: A plays your original mix (bypassed), B plays the master. Turn on MATCH first and play B for a few seconds (about 3 after Auto-Master or a new snapshot; after you change settings by hand it follows within about 20 seconds, or press RESET under the Output meter to make it learn afresh): the louder side is turned down so both play at the same loudness, and your original is kept from clipping. Then you compare tone and punch, not volume. On B the meters show the real master (on A, your original mix), never the matched level. MATCH switches itself off when you close Masterology, when you open the project again and during a normal export; for a Realtime Export, switch MATCH off and make sure B is on first.
  • MONO / PHONE: listening checks next to MATCH. MONO plays left + right together (club PAs, phones): anything that disappears is out of phase. PHONE plays your master like a phone speaker (mono, about 300 Hz to 8 kHz): does the 808 still come through? You hear its harmonics, not its sub; that's what Saturation is for. They work with A/B and MATCH, switch off when you close Masterology and are never in a normal export (for a Realtime Export, switch them off first). A red banner shows while any listening aid is on.
  • Listen (headphones button): on Saturation, Soft Clipper, Resonance Tamer, Maximizer and Dynamics, it plays only what that module adds or takes off, so you learn what it does. Quiet changes are turned up so you can hear them (the banner says by how much). On Dynamics, the S buttons solo one band. One at a time; BACK TO MASTER on the banner (or the button again) returns to the master.
  • Maximizer: makes the master loud. Drive pushes the mix into the limiter; Ceiling is the highest peak allowed (−1 dBTP, or −1.5 for Loud and Club masters, leaves room for streaming's MP3/AAC copies). Input Trim turns a mix that is already too loud down before the whole chain. Watch Short-term while you turn Drive.
  • Loudness: watch Short-term while you turn Drive (it follows the last 3 seconds). Integrated averages the whole time since it started, so it restarts a moment after you change a control (Settings). True Peak stays at or below the Ceiling.
  • Maximizer extras: Bass First (50–100 % for trap) lets the 808 take the limiting so the vocal and hats stop dipping on every 808 hit. Dither: set 16-bit only when you export a 16-bit WAV.
  • Unlimiter: for a beat, loop or mix that arrives already loud and flat (limited or clipped). It lifts the start of each hit so the drums punch again; the level between hits stays put. Squash shows how flattened your audio is: 12.5 dB or more is open (nothing to undo, it rests), 8.5 dB or less is brickwalled. Start at Amount 75 % and compare with MATCH.
  • Tonal EQ: turn a band's knob, or drag its numbered point on the spectrum: sideways moves the frequency, up/down sets the gain. Double-click a point to reset it. Master EQ moves of 1–3 dB go a long way. The button under each knob picks STEREO, MID (centre only) or SIDE (stereo edges only): try Air +1.5 dB on SIDE for width.
  • Stem EQ: turns the vocals, drums, bass (808 included) or everything else up or down inside your finished mix. Moves of 1–3 dB are usually all you need; flip MATCH on and A/B: the part you moved should step forward. Keep Loudness (on by default) holds the overall loudness, so a move changes the balance, not how loud the mix is. "Model Hears" shows how the AI divides your music right now. Auto-Master never changes these knobs; when your vocal sits unusually low or loud for the genre it suggests a Vocals move you can try with one Apply click.
  • Tone Steady: keeps the tone consistent through the song. Amount 40 % and Speed Slow steady a verse that gets muddier or a hook that gets harsher, without changing your overall sound (that's the Tonal EQ's job). Press LEARN (or run Auto-Master over the whole song) and it holds every part to your song's own overall tone, so what you hear from any start point is what you export. CLEAR makes it follow the last ~10 seconds of music again.
  • Resonance Tamer: finds ringing tones that stick out and cuts only those, only while they ring. Start at Amount 50 %; try Focus Harshness for piercing hats or vocals, Boxiness for a boxy low mid.
  • Bass Control: Mono Below at about 100 Hz makes the bass mono so it hits on club systems and phones. Sub Tame evens out 808 notes that jump out. Punch adds thump to the start of each kick.
  • Glue: one bus compressor for the whole mix, so the beat and the vocal move as one. Bus is clean and firm, Opto smooth and slow, Tube the softest. Try Ratio 2:1, Attack 10 ms, Release AUTO, Ignore Lows 90 Hz (keeps the 808 from pumping the vocal), then lower Threshold until the meter shows 1–2 dB on the hook.
  • Dynamics: four-band compression. Pick a band, raise its Ratio (2:1 is plenty), then lower the Threshold until the band's button shows 1–3 dB on the loud hits. Impact sets each band's punch (Low +30 % = harder kick); Sustain sets its tail length (Low −30 % = tighter 808s, +30 % = longer, fuller ones). The loudness stays about the same. The STEREO pill under the bands switches all bands to the centre only (MID ONLY) or the stereo edges only (SIDE ONLY).
  • Stereo Imager: width for lows, mids and highs. Keep the lows at 100 %; try the highs at 110–115 % and keep Correlation above +0.3.
  • Saturation: adds harmonics. Tape is smooth, Tube is rich (fuller bass), Clip is bright. Focus picks what gets them: Bass (the 808 shows up on phone speakers, vocals clean), Highs (sheen on hats) or Bass+Hi (both, vocal clean). Channels Mid keeps the harmonics in the centre, Side puts them only on the stereo edges. Aim for the Harmonics meter around −25 to −35 dB (Highs: −40 to −50).
  • Soft Clipper: turn it on, then lower Clip Level until the Clipping meter shows 1–3 dB on the drums. Clip Level is measured from the Maximizer ceiling, so peaks always use the full headroom.
  • Learn: the right panel explains what each control is doing to your track, live. Red boxes need a decision and say exactly what to do; blue boxes are tips.
