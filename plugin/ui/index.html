QUICK HELP
All eleven modules are live. Click one in the Signal Chain to open it; Learn explains every control.
  • Auto-Master: pick your genre (top), an intensity and a target, then play the loudest part of your song and press Auto-Master. It listens for 12 seconds, sets every module and explains why in Learn. The undo arrow puts everything back.
  • Match ref: click Match ref (next to the targets) and load a released song you want to sound like (WAV, AIFF, FLAC or MP3). Polisher measures its loudest part; then play the loudest part of your song and press Auto-Master: it aims for the reference's tone, loudness, width and low end instead of the genre's. The dashed line on the spectrum is the reference's tone. The × on the pill clears it.
  • Knobs: drag up/down. Hold Shift for fine control. Double-click to reset. Mouse wheel works too.
  • A / B: A plays your original mix (bypassed), B plays the master. Turn on MATCH first and play B for about 10 seconds: the louder side is turned down so both play at the same loudness, and your original is kept from clipping. Then you compare tone and punch, not volume. On B the meters show the real master (on A, your original mix), never the matched level. MATCH switches itself off when you close Polisher and during a normal export; for a Realtime Export, switch MATCH off and make sure B is on first.
  • Maximizer: makes the master loud. Drive pushes the mix into the limiter; Ceiling is the highest peak allowed (−1 dBTP, or −1.5 for loud masters, leaves room for streaming's MP3/AAC copies). Input Trim turns a mix that is already too loud down before the whole chain. Watch Short-term while you turn Drive.
  • Loudness: watch Short-term while you turn Drive (it follows the last 3 seconds). Integrated averages the whole time since it started, so it restarts a moment after you change a control (Settings). True Peak stays at or below the Ceiling.
  • Unlimiter: for a beat, loop or mix that arrives already loud and flat (limited or clipped). It lifts the start of each hit so the drums punch again; the level between hits stays put. Squash shows how flattened your audio is: 12.5 dB or more is open (nothing to undo, it rests), 8.5 dB or less is brickwalled. Start at Amount 75 % and compare with MATCH.
  • Tonal EQ: turn a band's knob, or drag its numbered point on the spectrum: sideways moves the frequency, up/down sets the gain. Double-click a point to reset it. Master EQ moves of 1–3 dB go a long way.
  • Stem EQ: turns the vocals, drums, bass (808 included) or everything else up or down inside your finished mix. Moves of 1–3 dB are usually all you need; flip MATCH on and A/B: the part you moved should step forward. Keep Loudness (on by default) holds the overall loudness, so a move changes the balance, not how loud the mix is. "Model Hears" shows how the AI divides your music right now. Auto-Master never changes these knobs; when your vocal sits unusually low or loud for the genre it suggests a Vocals move you can try with one Apply click.
  • Tone Steady: keeps the tone consistent through the song. Amount 40 % and Speed Slow steady a verse that gets muddier or a hook that gets harsher, without changing your overall sound (that's the Tonal EQ's job).
  • Resonance Tamer: finds ringing tones that stick out and cuts only those, only while they ring. Start at Amount 50 %; try Focus Harshness for piercing hats or vocals, Boxiness for a boxy low mid.
  • Bass Control: Mono Below at about 100 Hz makes the bass mono so it hits on club systems and phones. Sub Tame evens out 808 notes that jump out. Punch adds thump to the start of each kick.
  • Dynamics: four-band compression. Pick a band, raise its Ratio (2:1 is plenty), then lower the Threshold until the band's button shows 1–3 dB on the loud hits.
  • Stereo Imager: width for lows, mids and highs. Keep the lows at 100 %; try the highs at 110–115 % and keep Correlation above +0.3.
  • Saturation: adds harmonics. Tape is smooth, Tube is rich (fuller bass), Clip is bright. Focus Bass only puts them on the 808 alone, so it shows up on phone speakers while vocals stay clean. Aim for the Harmonics meter around −25 to −35 dB.
  • Soft Clipper: turn it on, then lower Clip Level until the Clipping meter shows 1–3 dB on the drums. Clip Level is measured from the Maximizer ceiling, so peaks always use the full headroom.
  • Learn: the right panel explains what each control is doing to your track, live. Red boxes need a decision and say exactly what to do; blue boxes are tips.
